Here are some of my current and past projects:

Along-strike Variations in Seismicity in the Central Andes (ongoing)

The Andes Mountain Range exhibits distinct along-strike variations in crustal thickness, shortening and uplift. Two dense arrays of three-component nodal and broadband seismometers were deployed at ~23-24˚S and ~35-36˚S as a part of the TransANdean Great Orogeny (TANGO) project to understand the driving processes for the along-strike variations in Andean deformation. These two datasets are used to build an earthquake catalog of each of those two regions using a machine-learning-based workflow. This study focuses on identification of key differences in seismicity as revealed by the new earthquake catalogs, which also reflects the variation in deformation processes that resulted in the along-strike variations in the Andes.

Crustal Imaging of the Southern Central Andes by Seismic Autocorrelation (ongoing)

Autocorrelation of seismic waveform or ambient noise data recorded on individual seismic stations can reveal subsurface reflectivity underneath the station. In this study, to image the major subsurface boundaries, this processing method is implemented on the data collected by a dense array of three-component nodal and broadband seismometers at ~23-24˚S, deployed as part of the TANGO project. The autocorrelation traces are used to identify major subsurface boundaries within the different tectonic units including the Moho, basal detachments in the Eastern Cordillera fold-and-thrust belt, and crustal magma bodies in the volcanic arc and the Puna plateau.

Seismicity and Present-day Crustal Deformation in the Southern Puna Plateau

In this study, the upper crustal structure and present-day deformation in the Southern Puna Plateau is investigated using a new regional earthquake catalog derived with a deep-learning-based phase picker. Results show an order of magnitude more events than the previous study, abundant strike-slip seismicity at shallow depths in the eastern Southern Puna plateau, a broad zone of seismic quiescence in the western plateau, a complex stress field in the region separating eastern and western plateau, and a well-constrained seismic swarm. The study is now published in the Journal of Geophysical Research: Solid Earth [LINK].

Teleseismic Source Modeling of Strong-to-major Himalayan Earthquakes

This study was part of my Master’s Thesis, where I analyzed source mechanisms of earthquakes along the Himalayan arc over 30 years to understand regional variations in tectonic processes. Using multiple inversion techniques which include finite-fault modeling, point-source moment tensor inversion, and probabilistic bootstrapping inversion, I modeled the earthquake source processes and compared those along cross-sectional profiles across the Himalayas.
